## Candidate legitimate-interests assessment

The [ICO three-part approach](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/how-do-we-apply-legitimate-interests-in-practice/) requires purpose, necessity and balancing. Each activity needs its own conclusion; the portfolio purpose does not automatically justify all third-party activity.

## Optional precise location and statutory correspondence

Proposed basis for the operator's optional precise-location processing: consent. An informed affirmative location-button action precedes the browser permission request. Manual location remains available. Stop/manual edit clears coordinates and invalidates pending position/geocoding responses; withdrawal cannot undo a request already sent. Deployed tests used synthetic public coordinates plus real Google geocoding and verified withdrawal, denial/unavailability and cancellation. A native OS permission test and owner review of the explanation/choice remain required. Do not store coordinates as a consent audit record; retain the notice version and implementation/test description instead.

For processing necessary to meet statutory data-subject-rights and privacy-complaint duties, compliance with the applicable legal obligation is proposed. Record the relevant duty and deadline in a private case record; do not label every general enquiry a statutory request. Minimal handling records have the adopted twelve-month review/deletion point after closure, with justified monthly-reviewed exceptions for active investigations/claims. Verify against [current ICO complaint guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/how-to-deal-with-data-protection-complaints/what-do-we-do-when-we-receive-a-complaint/).

## Provider roles and transfers — evidence needed

Published Maps terms incorporate controller–controller terms. The public terms identify Google LLC as UK end controller; this is not proof of the accepted account version or a special agreement. Netlify's DPA applies to on-behalf processing; independent provider purposes may have different roles. Record the relevant service, data, recipient role, agreement and current version for each activity.

Published provider documents describe US/international processing and transfer frameworks. Before a transfer conclusion, identify actual recipients/destinations, check current applicable UK adequacy/certification coverage or contractual safeguards, onward processing and any required transfer risk assessment. Provider policy links and a billing country are not a completed assessment. See [provider questions and the adopted procedure](privacy-operations.md).

## Storage/access — technical inventory completed, decisions pending

Source inspection found no Ravenous cookies or storage writes for search history. The older authenticated profile pass observed a private-access session cookie. The enforcing production pass observed one local-storage key named `nl-hud:owner-private:v1`, consistent with the documented Netlify owner toolbar preference; no session-storage, IndexedDB or Cache Storage entries were present. Existing-profile counts cannot establish fresh-profile or third-party behaviour.

The [clean-profile comparison](clean-browser-assessment-2026-10-06.md) is now complete for authenticated private production, with cookies allowed and all cross-site cookies blocked. The tested flows worked in both modes. App local/session storage, IndexedDB and Cache Storage were empty; a Session, Secure, HttpOnly `nf_edge` cookie was present for private access. Captured Maps/Places/photo requests had no cookie counts; photos redirected to the Google image host. HTTP caching and initial SDK/telemetry requests were observed separately. The clean profile did not create the older owner-toolbar key.

Private authentication is a candidate strictly necessary use, subject to purpose limitation; it is separate from public discovery. No persistent advertising/analytics storage was identified in these flows. This does not exclude other device-access methods or settle Google's secondary purposes. Apply the [ICO exceptions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/) purpose by purpose, obtaining consent before any non-exempt activity. Initial loading and autocomplete necessity/balancing decisions above remain pending. Preserve provider enquiries and check the final public-visitor configuration when login/toolbar is removed; do not make the site public merely to test it.
